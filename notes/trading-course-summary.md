# Trading Course Transcript — Condensed Notes

> Summary of a ~10-hour forex trading YouTube tutorial. See the caveat at the
> bottom: the technical-analysis vocabulary is standard retail material, but the
> demonstrated risk management is dangerous and the video ends in a paid-mentorship
> sales funnel. Treat as notes on what was claimed, not as endorsed advice.

## 1. Markets Overview
- **Forex** (~$7.5–8T daily volume) — largest, most liquid, open 24/5. The presenter's primary market.
- **Stocks** (~$200–300B), **Commodities** (~$100B), **Crypto** (~$100–200B, volatile, only market where you own the asset).
- You never own the underlying in forex/CFDs — you're betting on price direction only.
- **Currency pairs**: base vs. quote (e.g., EUR/USD). Pair rises = base strengthening; falls = quote strengthening. Stick to majors (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, etc.) for liquidity, tight spreads, cleaner moves.

## 2. Trader Types
| Type | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Position ("whale") | 1–2 trades/month | Large capital, long holds |
| Swing | 4–5/month | Best risk-to-reward, ~60–65% win rate |
| Day | 2–4/week | ~40–50% win rate |
| Scalper | 2–3/day | Advised against — overexposure |

He trades a swing/day hybrid. Core idea: fewer, higher-quality trades — "you make money by entering right and letting it ride, not by being involved."

## 3. Tools & Platforms
- **TradingView** — all analysis/charting (candlestick + line charts only; "no gap candlesticks" indicator; hide volume).
- **Broker** — holds funds, routes orders (he promotes high-leverage offshore brokers — see caveat).
- **MetaTrader 5** — execution only (lot size, SL, TP). Order types: market execution (his default), buy/sell limits & stops (he avoids — no confirmation at entry).
- **Forex Factory** — economic calendar; he treats fundamentals as a "mystery box" and trades technicals, claiming news mostly follows the technical trend.

## 4. Trading Sessions
- Trade only **pre-London through mid-New York (~1:00 AM–10:30 AM EST)**; London/NY overlap = peak volatility.
- Avoid Sydney/Tokyo (low volume) and late NY (dying volume + overnight swap fees).

## 5. Market Structure (core of the method)
- **Price action is everything**; candle bodies define structure, wicks are just history.
- **Bullish** = higher highs (HH) + higher lows (HL). **Bearish** = lower lows (LL) + lower highs (LH).
- Structure only shifts on a **body close** beyond the HL/LH — wicks don't count, and a candle must close to confirm.
- New HH always creates a new HL (and vice versa). Find it with the **"snake trick"**: trace back from the most recent extreme on a line chart; the first significant turn is the new HL/LH.
- Use the **line chart** to identify structure cleanly, then transfer levels to candle bodies.

## 6. Top-Down Analysis
- Trend timeframes: **Weekly, Daily, 4H** (lookback: ~5–6 yrs weekly, ~6–12 mo on 4H).
- Entry timeframes: 2H, 1H, 30m, 15m — used only at the very end.
- Require **two consecutive timeframes in sync** (Weekly+Daily or Daily+4H) before considering a trade. Trade only *with* the trend ("the trend is your friend").
- Only analyze price *inside* the current HH/HL (or LH/LL) range — everything outside is noise.

## 7. Areas of Interest (AOI) = Support/Resistance = Supply/Demand = Order Blocks
- All the same concept: a zone price has repeatedly reacted to.
- **Valid AOI = minimum 3 touches** (any mix of support/resistance reactions, body-based, not wicks). More touches = stronger.
- Size: **max 60 pips, min 5; sweet spot 20–35**.
- Only mark AOIs **on Weekly and Daily**, and only inside the current structure range. Overlapping Weekly+Daily AOIs are strongest.
- Rules: **buy at support, sell at resistance — never the reverse.** Only enter *at* an AOI. If price breaks below your buy zone, wait for a break-and-retest from the other side.

## 8. Continuation & Reversal Patterns
- **Break and retest** (his favorite continuation): body close through a level → price returns to retest it → enter with rejection confirmation. Entering on the raw breakout is allowed but weaker.
- **Head & Shoulders** (his only reversal pattern): valid **only after the neckline breaks** (= structure shift). The neckline is the prior structure level/AOI, not a drawn diagonal. Proper entry = break of neckline → retest → rejection → enter. Right-shoulder entries are high-risk. Inverted H&S = same logic for buys. Ignore Fibonacci, pitchforks, harmonics, etc.

## 9. Candlestick Signals (entry confirmations, only at AOIs)
- **Doji / spinning top** — indecision, slowdown at a level.
- **Hammer / shooting star** — wick rejection of a level.
- **Engulfing candle** — must engulf the bodies of the prior two candles; his key entry trigger.
- **Morning/Evening Star** — doji/rejection + engulfing combo; his favorite formation.
- Higher timeframe = stronger signal. These patterns appear everywhere — they only matter at key levels with the trend.

## 10. Confluence Trading & the Trading Plan
- Stack reasons: **trend (2+ TFs) → AOI → pattern (H&S / break-retest) → entry signal (engulfing/star)** → optional 50 EMA (dynamic S/R, "cherry on top," lowest priority).
- More confluences = lower risk = where you size up. "High risk ≠ high reward."
- No trade without the checklist met; if price never reaches your zone, **no trade** — move on.

## 11. Risk & Trade Management
- Pre-calculate lot size from account balance, % risk, and SL pips (broker position-size calculators). SL and TP set before entry.
- **Minimum 1:2 risk-to-reward**, aim for 1:4+ ("a free second trade").
- **Set and forget**: once in, let it hit SL or TP; don't micromanage off P&L — "the charts drive the numbers, not the other way around."
- One-and-done: win or lose, stop for the week (his rule during the challenge).
- Accept wick-outs as inevitable; accountability rituals for breaking the plan (e.g., his "punishment Beetle").

## 12. The $100→$1M Challenge (his account-flip method)
- Not literally $100→$1M: a chain of compounded flips ($100→$400→$3.2k→$8k→$15k→…→$1M), risking **100% early**, tapering to ~35–50% per trade later, one selective trade per week.
- He admits multiple failed attempts and a blown ~$330k account before completing it.

## Caveat
The structure/AOI/confluence material in sections 5–10 is standard retail
technical analysis — fine as vocabulary. Sections 11–12 as *demonstrated*
(50–100% risk per trade, 1:500 offshore leverage, unregulated brokers)
contradict the video's own "low-risk" framing and are the dangerous part;
conventional practice is 0.5–2% risk per trade at a regulated broker. The
profit claims are unverifiable, and the video funnels viewers into a paid
mentorship and affiliate broker signups.
